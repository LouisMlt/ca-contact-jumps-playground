Why can small α look straighter?

The horizontal coordinate is X=(log n)^alpha. As α approaches zero, X=1+alpha log log n+O(alpha²). The axes fit each view. Straightness is not monotone in α for arbitrary data or k. This chapter uses Y=rho(n)^k; the classical prime-switch chapter instead uses y=log rho(n).

FROM A PICTURE TO A THEOREM

Why skipping points
changes the arithmetic.

A visual route through the argument. Open a step for the mathematical connection.

First, let primes build the numbers.

Classical CA integers maximise rho(n)/n^epsilon, where rho(n)=sigma(n)/n. The threshold F(p,a) tells us when the exponent of p increases. Equality retains both neighbouring choices.

Then, let geometry choose the contacts.

Fix an integer k ≥ 1 and 0 < α < 1. Plot X(n) = (ln n)α and Y(n) = ρ(n)k. A contact maximises Y(n) − λX(n) for some λ ≥ 0 against every CA competitor n ≥ 55440. The reference 55440 stays a competitor but is excluded from the contact set. The formal argument proves that consecutive contacts divide one another.

A finite plotted upper boundary only supplies candidates: later, unplotted points may invalidate a line.

A clean prime cluster makes the jump.

A clean cluster gives successive CA states Nᵢ = N₀p₁⋯pᵢ. The proof's slope estimate makes their successive slopes increase, so interior states lie below a chord and cannot be global contacts. Consecutive contacts bracket the block, and divisibility transfers its distinct primes into their quotient.

Maynard's quantitative prime-cluster theorem supplies enough windows to survive the sparse higher-power exceptions. The linked Lean development proves the connected implication with that theorem as an explicit hypothesis.

Must every sequence of slopes increase?

No. The estimate applies to sufficiently large clean blocks with the proof's hypotheses. Arbitrary neighbouring CA integers need not satisfy it. The optional P-labelled schematic is a constructed geometric diagram, not a CA sequence; the clean-block chapter separately illustrates its required increasing slopes.

Arbitrarily many primes.
Beyond every starting point.

Consecutive contacts divide; their quotients have arbitrarily many distinct prime factors.

Scope and exact quantifiers

For fixed k≥1 and 0<alpha<1, for every requested M and starting index J, there is a j ≥ J. Here ω counts distinct prime factors. The endpoint α = 1 is outside the theorem. The linked Lean development retains Maynard's quantitative theorem as an explicit external hypothesis.
